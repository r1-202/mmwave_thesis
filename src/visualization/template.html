MSc Thesis · Lebanese International University · 2025

Machine Learning–Based Prediction & Optimization of Weather-Induced Attenuation in 5G mmWave Links

A complete AI research system trained on 512,944 rows of real measured 38 GHz attenuation from a commercial microwave link (CoMMon campaign). Six machine-learning predictors are benchmarked against the traditional ITU-R P.838-3 and Crane (1980) physical models, with hybrid-beamforming link optimization and an interactive Lebanon 5G coverage projection. Every figure below is computed directly from the dataset and the project's own physics code.

07 · Coverage Projection

🇱🇧 Lebanon 5G mmWave Coverage Map

10 cities · interactive

Per-city 28/60 GHz attenuation, SNR, throughput and availability for a representative 1 km small-cell link. Click any marker for the full breakdown.

Methodology & honesty note. The CoMMon dataset is a single 38 GHz link time-series and contains no geographic coordinates, so a per-city map cannot be read directly from it. Each city below is therefore a transparent model projection: the project's own ITU-R P.838-3 (rain) + P.676 (gaseous) attenuation functions and the link-budget / beamforming simulation are evaluated at each city's representative ITU-R rain rate (the wet Mediterranean coast vs. the dry Bekaa rain-shadow gradient). Headline ML metrics (RMSE, R², MAE) are unchanged and come straight from training on the real measured attenuation.
